RETRIEVAL DIAGNOSTIC 1
============================================================

Query:
"What is the official language?"

BM25:
- The relevant constitutional chunk was ranked #1.
- Chunk ID: 19
- Page: 2
- Source: Algeria Constitution.pdf
- BM25 score: 14.905290

Relevant retrieved content:
"Article 3 Arabic is the national and official language.
Tamazight is also a national language."

The BM25 retriever therefore successfully identified the primary
constitutional source containing the answer.

RRF:
- Chunk ID 19 remained rank #1.
- RRF score: 0.016667
- BM25 rank: #1
- Dense rank: not reported by the diagnostic.

Observation:
The lexical retrieval component performs strongly for this query
because the query terms "official" and "language" occur directly in
the relevant constitutional passage.

The result also demonstrates that the generic chunks are capable of
preserving enough surrounding context for a simple factual legal
question.


============================================================
RETRIEVAL DIAGNOSTIC 2
============================================================

Query:
"What is the religion of the country?"

BM25:
- The most relevant constitutional passage was not ranked first.
- The top results were largely from the Algeria gender-justice
  document and other unrelated legal material.
- The constitutional chunk containing the direct answer appeared
  lower in the ranking.

Important relevant result:
- Chunk ID: 19
- Page: 2
- Source: Algeria Constitution.pdf
- Content contains:
  "Article 2 Islam is the religion of the State."

The constitutional chunk was ranked #17 by BM25 in this diagnostic,
despite containing the exact answer.

Observation:
This exposes a weakness of pure lexical retrieval. The query asks
for the country's religion, while the authoritative source expresses
the concept as "religion of the State". The lexical mismatch causes
BM25 to assign high scores to unrelated documents containing terms
such as "religion", "Islamic", or other semantically related legal
language.


============================================================
RRF OBSERVATION
============================================================

The current RRF implementation combines the ranked BM25 and dense
retrieval results.

For the query:

"What is the official language?"

the constitutional chunk containing Article 3 was successfully
promoted to the top of the final ranking.

For the query:

"What is the religion of the country?"

the final diagnostic did not demonstrate a strong dense-retrieval
contribution in the displayed ranking, and BM25-dominated results
remained prominent.

This indicates that the current hybrid retrieval implementation is
functional, but the contribution of dense retrieval should be
investigated further.

In particular, the diagnostic output should explicitly expose:

- dense rank
- dense distance/similarity
- BM25 rank
- BM25 score
- RRF contribution from each retriever
- final RRF score

This would make it possible to determine whether dense retrieval is
actually improving ranking or whether RRF is effectively behaving
like a BM25-first system for the current queries.


============================================================
CURRENT RETRIEVAL FINDINGS
============================================================

1. BM25 is highly effective when the query vocabulary closely matches
   the wording of the source.

2. BM25 can fail when the wording of the question differs from the
   wording used in the legal document.

3. The query "What is the religion of the country?" demonstrates this
   limitation clearly: the authoritative constitutional passage says
   "religion of the State", and BM25 initially ranks several unrelated
   chunks above it.

4. The generic chunking strategy is sufficient to retrieve the relevant
   constitutional passage for some simple factual queries, but it does
   not explicitly model legal structure such as Articles, Chapters,
   Titles, or sections.

5. Dense retrieval is intended to compensate for lexical mismatch,
   which is one of the main motivations for the hybrid BM25 + dense
   retrieval architecture.

6. RRF provides a simple rank-based mechanism for combining lexical and
   semantic retrieval without requiring the scores of the two retrieval
   systems to be calibrated to the same scale.

7. The current experiments are preliminary because the evaluation set
   contains only two questions. The results should therefore be treated
   as an initial diagnostic rather than a statistically robust
   evaluation.


============================================================
CURRENT LIMITATIONS
============================================================

- Very small evaluation set.
- Generic chunking rather than legal structure-aware segmentation.
- Dense retrieval contribution requires further diagnostic analysis.
- Retrieval diagnostics do not yet expose all individual RRF
  contributions.
- The current corpus contains heterogeneous legal documents with
  different structures and purposes.
- The current evaluation focuses on a small number of factual questions.


============================================================
NEXT STEP
============================================================

The next planned improvement is structure-aware legal document
segmentation.

The planned approach will preserve legal structures such as:

- Constitution
- Title
- Chapter
- Article
- Section

rather than treating the document as a sequence of generic text
chunks.

The goal is to investigate whether legal-structure-aware segmentation
improves retrieval precision and reduces irrelevant context.

This improvement is intentionally marked as future/in progress and is
NOT included in the current benchmark.

The current benchmark therefore represents:

    Generic Chunking
        +
    BM25
        +
    Dense Retrieval
        +
    RRF

and serves as the baseline/current snapshot against which future
structure-aware retrieval experiments can be compared.
